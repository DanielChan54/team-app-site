10 Best Practices for Managing Remote Teams

Ethan Walsh
|
July 5, 2026

Managing a remote team comes with unique opportunities and challenges. While flexible work arrangements can improve employee satisfaction and productivity, they also require thoughtful communication, clear expectations, and the right collaboration tools.

Whether your team works remotely full-time or follows a hybrid schedule, these best practices can help create a more connected, productive, and successful workplace.

1. Establish Clear Communication Channels

Remote teams rely heavily on digital communication. Make it easy for employees to know where to ask questions, share updates, and collaborate on projects.

Using dedicated channels for different topics helps reduce confusion and ensures important information doesn't get lost in lengthy conversations.

2. Set Clear Expectations

Employees perform their best when they understand what's expected of them.

Clearly define goals, responsibilities, deadlines, and priorities for every project. Regular check-ins help ensure everyone stays aligned without the need for constant supervision.

3. Focus on Outcomes, Not Hours

Successful remote teams measure results rather than time spent online.

Giving employees the flexibility to manage their schedules encourages autonomy while keeping everyone accountable for delivering high-quality work.

Trust often leads to stronger performance than micromanagement.

4. Encourage Collaboration

Remote work shouldn't mean working alone.

Create opportunities for employees to brainstorm ideas, solve problems together, and share knowledge. Collaboration strengthens relationships and often leads to better solutions than individual work alone.

5. Keep Meetings Purposeful

Meetings should have a clear objective and agenda.

Whenever possible, replace unnecessary meetings with written updates or asynchronous communication. This gives employees more uninterrupted time to focus while keeping everyone informed.

When meetings are necessary, keep them concise and action-oriented.

6. Invest in the Right Tools

Technology plays a critical role in remote collaboration.

Choosing a platform that combines messaging, task management, file sharing, and scheduling helps reduce friction and keeps projects moving forward. Having everything in one place also makes onboarding new employees much easier.

7. Build Strong Team Relationships

Great teams aren't built solely through work.

Encourage opportunities for employees to connect socially through virtual coffee chats, team celebrations, or informal conversations. Building relationships strengthens trust and creates a more enjoyable work environment.

8. Support Employee Well-Being

Remote work can blur the line between professional and personal life.

Encourage healthy work habits by respecting time zones, promoting regular breaks, and reminding employees to disconnect after working hours. Supporting work-life balance helps prevent burnout and improves long-term productivity.

9. Ask for Feedback Regularly

Every team is different, and no workflow is perfect.

Regular feedback allows managers to identify challenges, improve processes, and ensure employees feel heard. Small improvements based on employee input often lead to meaningful long-term results.

10. Continuously Improve Your Processes

Remote work continues to evolve, and successful teams evolve with it.

Review your workflows regularly, experiment with new ideas, and stay open to adopting tools and strategies that improve collaboration. A mindset of continuous improvement helps teams stay adaptable as their needs change.

Final Thoughts

Managing a remote team successfully isn't about monitoring every activity—it's about creating an environment where people can communicate effectively, collaborate easily, and focus on meaningful work.

By building trust, establishing clear processes, and providing the right tools, organizations can create remote teams that are productive, engaged, and prepared for long-term success.
